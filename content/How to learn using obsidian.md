1. We should learn as much as we can about the world
2. We retain what we know by remembering
3. To remember, information has to be written down

There are so many daily thoughts and insights that are immediately forgotten. We should all be writing these down, but spending precious time of our life being library keepers of our brains isn't fun.

From what I've seen in [No Boilerplate's youtube videos](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLZaoyhMXgBzp9blLuIrwr5eRgBdnPy3BM), and from what I've read in [[A System for Writing - A Zettelkasten Primer]] by Bob Doto, [[Zettelkasten]] seems to be the most efficient and pain free way to capture, store and retrieve my thoughts.

The workflow:

1. Capture
2. Process
3. write

---

# 1) Capture

When an idea pops into my head, I don't want to drop everything for 5 minutes just to write it down. What I do is quickly write just enough to be able to later pick up where i left off.

## Fleeting notes

- notes just to remember something, then discarded
- highlights from inner monologue

## Reference notes

- quotes and ideas from articles, videos, podcasts, books
- make sure to mark down page number, few word summary
- put them into [[Inbox]], later process into proper references and atomic notes
- you don't need to understand an ideas relevance to your thinking to cite it in a reference note [[A System for Writing - A Zettelkasten Primer|(p46)]]

knowledge gathering != understanding
fleeting notes need to be processed to keep them concise and relevant

---

# 2) Process

transform captured thoughts into atomic notes

## Atomic notes

- high cohesion, low coupling
  - work well together, don't need one to understand another
- should be as simple as possible
  - teachable unit of knowledge

### how to create an atomic note

- title - enough to know what it is in a week
- body - enough to know what it is in a few years
- tags - atomic tag, topic tag
- links to as many other atomic, reference and other notes in body
- references - links outside of obsidian

### tips for refining notes

[[A System for Writing - A Zettelkasten Primer]]:

- interrogate the content of your ideas.
- read the subtext.
- stretch their relevancy.
- flip them over and turn them inside out.
- don(t forget to explain why links are made, not just linkdump
- title should be declarative not descriptive
  - not all apples are edible > apples and edibility
- write aditional notes in light of and in spite of previous ones

## Spaced repetition plugin

- best way to not get drowned in a sea of fleeting notes
- use it to review fleeting notes
  - make sure to check in at least once a week
  - if it takes less than 5 minutes do it now and delete
  - otherwise do now or set difficulty and save for later
- use it to review atomic and reference notes
  - to allow for adding links to existing notes using new context

---

# 3) Write

1. Create outline out of atomic notes
2. ...
3. profit?

- Leavarage atomic notes as much as possible
- Keep people reading (from [How Veritasium gets views](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QHhJ8_TJeNo))
  - people don't pay attention when they think they know what you're talking about
  - title isn't supposed to be topic, it's supposed to capture interest, answer a question
  1. misconception
  2. question first explanation after
  3. technical a plot interesting b plot (not as relevant to writing?)
