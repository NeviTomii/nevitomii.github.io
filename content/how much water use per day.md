what you may have read online is that AI datacenters use anywhere between 10 mililitres and a bottle of water per prompt generated (depending on who you ask), or that by 2030, AI is projected to use [as much water as 1.3 billion people](https://time.com/article/2026/06/03/ai-global-water-resources-un-report/). What I learned from that is absolutely nothing. I had no idea how much water tech industries usually use or if water consumption of a billion people is a lot. But everyone else seemed very alarmed by those numbers, so I decided to look into it.

One of the most widely cited reports on world water stocks comes from [Igor A. Shiklomanov 1993](https://www.quarks.de/wp-content/uploads/Water_in_Crisis_Chapter_2_Oxford_Univers.pdf), who compiled the available data in rough estimates. Of all the water on Earth, approximately 97,5% is salt water and only 2,5% is freshwater. Around two thirds of freshwater are locked away in glaciers and ice caps, while roughly one third is stored as groundwater.

However, not all groundwater is practically usable. Deep groundwater reserves can be difficult and expensive to extract, and many deep aquifers contain high concentrations of dissolved minerals and salts. I could not find reliable data on economically accessible groundwater, so generously assuming half of it is reachable and adding in lakes and rivers we come to a grand total of 5,5 milion km3 or 0,40% of total water on earth.

this is interesting on its own, but not very telling. even if we 10x our water consumption, the refill rate of all this water capacity remains the same - the rainfall over land

This doesn't mean only that fraction of water is usable, but rather that only a small fraction is available without major technical and economic constraints. We also have to keep in mind the refill rate - while the raw amount of water available is large, to keep the water system stable in it's current state, we can only extract water up to the rate at which it is returned via precipitation and not evaporated - [44 211 km3 (page 63)](https://openknowledge.fao.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/06e745be-89a5-4850-b819-10670efc1160/content)

At the 2018 level of water withdrawal - 4079 km3/year we are already using almost 10% of yearly land water flow. This might not sound as an issue, but considering how unevenly distributed rainfall is, water overconsumption is already a major contributor to droughts and water shortages. this is only going to get worse. Climate change increases the risk of serious droughts by amplyfing extreme weather events and the need for sustainable water usage will rise.

# concentrating water intensive agriculture in water rich countries -- using water footprint and virtual water

most pressure on the world's water resources comes from agriculture. It accounts to 72% water usage globaly. increasing productivity of water used in agriculture is crucial for avoiding water shortages and achieving food security. One way of achieving that is to encourage production of low-virtual-water crops. It is estimated that by 2050 we will need to produce roughly 50% more food.
