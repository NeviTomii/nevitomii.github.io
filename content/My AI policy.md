I never let AI dictate what this blog is about. I do let it rephrase my middle-school-level sentences so that reading this is tolerable for more people than just me.

What that means practically is that I find something interesting to write about, read until I'm confident I can defend my claims, attempt to write a post without help, and, after failing a few times, ask an agent to make the language bearable while keeping all the information intact.

This is what AI (and intelligence in general) should be for. People come up with all sorts of ideas, ranging from terrible to decent, and we should decide which is which based on their merit, not on whether they arrive in a pretty paragraph.

That being said, AI slop is still AI slop. By that I mean prompting an agent to "write an article about x". Current AI agents have a very distinct writing style, and when they take something already written online and repackage it as an article, there isn't much interesting or new to be learned from the result. Having AI run an opinionated blog under my name just misses the point.

# Morality of AI

while talking about this, I also want to touch on the way people are treated for using AI. If someone is against AI for whatever reason, that's their own choice. What feels strange to me is how much hostility there is towards people who don't share that opinion.

It makes me sad to see so many people pile on [Hank Green](https://www.hankgreen.com/) after his ["confession"](https://x.com/hankgreen/status/2083218912599212209) of using ChatGPT to find research papers. Even if all these people are against any form of AI, I struggle to see how they can feel justified in judging others so harshly.

For me personally, when I see AI used in restaurant photos, it discourages me from dining there because it suggests a lack of care for their business. But I wouldn't say they're wrong for posting an AI image or choosing to promote themselves that way. It's my preference, not a moral rule they have to follow.
